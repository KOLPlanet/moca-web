To choose an influencer marketing agency in Indonesia in 2026, pick the one that can show you, in writing, how it handles disclosure, product rules and creator vetting. Only then compare creator fit, cost structure and reporting. This checklist is for marketing leads at brands and apps entering Indonesia, including brands expanding from China. It covers four ways to source creators, the rules an agency should already know, 12 questions to ask, and the red flags that should end a pitch.

An influencer marketing agency finds and briefs creators, runs campaigns and, in Indonesia, should check content against advertising and product rules before it goes live.

New obligations also take effect on fixed dates. Indonesia's halal deadline for cosmetics falls on 17 October 2026. Guidance from BPOM, Indonesia's food and drug authority, says cosmetics sold in the country must hold a halal certificate [no later than that date](https://pmpuotskk.pom.go.id/berita/kewajiban-halal-kosmetik-oktober-2026-regulasi-bpjph-dan-bpom-semakin-terintegrasi), and the halal agency BPJPH says certification is [mandatory after it](https://bpjph.halal.go.id/read/bpjph-produk-kosmetik-wajib-bersertifikat-halal-pada-oktober-2026). For a brand briefing creators on a skincare launch, that is one more rule the agency has to check before a video goes live.

## Why does agency choice matter so much in Indonesia?

The audience is large and spread across several platforms. [DataReportal's Digital 2026 Indonesia report](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-indonesia) counts 230 million internet users at the end of 2025, or 80.5% of the population, and 180 million social media user identities in October 2025. Its late-2025 advertising reach figures put TikTok at 180 million adults, YouTube at 151 million and Instagram at 108 million. Those are ad-reach audiences rather than monthly active users, but they show how many creators and audience segments an agency has to sort through.

Creator content in Indonesia is also tied directly to purchases. [Google, Temasek and Bain's e-Conomy SEA 2025 report](https://blog.google/intl/id-id/company-news/outreach-initiatives/e-conomy-sea-2025-ekonomi-digital-indonesia-mendekati-gmv-us100-miliar/) puts Indonesia's 2025 digital economy at close to US$100 billion in GMV, with e-commerce at US$71 billion. Video commerce transactions rose 90% to 2.6 billion and the number of sellers grew 75% to 800,000, which the report says makes Indonesia the largest and fastest-growing video-commerce market in Southeast Asia. A weak creator brief now costs sales as well as reputation.

According to [We Are Social's commentary on the Digital 2026 report](https://wearesocial.com/id/blog/2025/11/digital-2026-top-digital-and-social-media-trends-in-indonesia/), Indonesians spend 21 hours 50 minutes a week on social media. Among internet users, 38.3% discover brands through search engines and 37.3% through social media ads. We Are Social also reports that influencer marketing spend rose 14.4% in 2025, without disclosing its method.

## Agency, marketplace, MCN or in-house: which sourcing model fits?

Brands entering Indonesia can source creators in four ways. The main difference between them is who carries the briefing, vetting, compliance review and payments. Deloitte Digital's Nick Purdon, [quoted by the World Federation of Advertisers in September 2026](https://wfanet.org/knowledge/item/2026/09/17/influencing-the-influencers), described the problem this way: "The challenge isn't creating more content, it's identifying the right creators and the right conversations."

MOCA Technology runs a full-service team and a creator marketplace, [KOLPlanet](https://www.kolplanet.com), so two of the four columns describe models MOCA sells. We wrote the table to point you to the right model even when MOCA does not offer it.

| Criterion | Full-service agency | Creator marketplace platform | Talent management or MCN | In-house team |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Control over creator choice | Agency proposes, brand approves | Brand picks from listed creators | Limited to the network's roster | Full |

| Compliance handling | Should review scripts against EPI, BPOM and platform rules; confirm in the contract | Mostly with the brand unless the platform offers review | Varies; check what the network reviews | Needs local legal and regulatory knowledge |

The models can be combined. A brand might use an agency for a regulated launch, then move always-on content to a marketplace once its brief and review steps are settled. Our earlier guide on [the pitfalls of influencer marketing in Indonesia](/news/how-to-drive-real-growth-in-indonesia-with-influencer-marketing-avoid-pitfalls-unlock-lasting-value) covers the campaign mistakes that apply to every model.

> **Key finding:** Every sourcing model in Indonesia still needs someone to review creator compliance. Full-service agencies take on briefing and script review, creator marketplaces such as KOLPlanet leave more of it with the brand, and in-house teams need local regulatory knowledge. Choose by who can review claims in Bahasa Indonesia.

## Which Indonesian rules should an influencer agency already know?

The rules that shape influencer content come from the national advertising code, product regulators, and laws on consumers, commerce, data, children and tax. The Consumer Protection Law, [UU 8/1999](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/33784/UU%20Nomor%208%20Tahun%201999.pdf), bans misleading ads in Article 17. Under Article 62, a misleading ad can bring up to five years in prison or a Rp2 billion fine, and other advertising violations up to two years or Rp500 million.

BPOM found that the share of non-compliant cosmetics ads rose from 21.63% in 2023 to 26.12% in 2024, according to its [February 2025 press release](https://www.pom.go.id/siaran-pers/bpom-tegaskan-aturan-penandaan-promosi-dan-iklan-kosmetik-wajib-dilaksanakan-pelaku-usaha). The [WFA Global Influencer Marketing Guidance (2025)](https://acaweb.ca/en/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2025/05/WFA_Global-Guidance-Influencer-Marketing-.pdf) says brands can be held at least jointly responsible when an influencer fails to comply, that intermediaries such as agencies may also be liable, and that platform disclosure tools may not be sufficient on their own.

## What should you ask an influencer agency before signing?

Ask each shortlisted agency these 12 questions and get the answers in writing. If the first five answers are vague, expect the compliance work to fall to your team.

1. **Which of our product claims need a regulator's approval, and who checks scripts against them?** Cosmetics claims must match the BPOM notification, processed food claims must match the registered label, and influencers may not promote medicines except as actors in an ad. A good answer names the reviewer and the step in the workflow.
2. **How do you set disclosure on each platform?** TikTok requires the commercial content disclosure toggle and says undisclosed promotional content may not be eligible for the For You feed ([TikTok Creator Academy](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/en/article/create-branded-content)). YouTube asks creators to select paid promotion so a label appears ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/154235?hl=en)). Instagram requires the paid partnership label on all branded content ([Instagram Help](https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/1123581461537025/)). Ask whether creators also add a text label such as #sponsor, the example in the EPI notes. Our overview of [platform policy changes on TikTok, YouTube and Instagram](/news/platform-policy-tightening-2025-how-tiktok-youtube-instagram-are-reshaping-the-creator-economy) gives more context.
3. **How do you vet a creator before the shortlist?** Ask which checks run on past sponsored posts, audience location and age, and earlier brand-safety problems.
4. **Who moderates comments on campaign posts, and how fast?** Komdigi says about 52% of online-gambling spam comments target regional influencer accounts, because their audiences are seen as more trusting and engaged and their comment moderation is relatively weaker ([Komdigi, July 2026](https://portal.komdigi.go.id/kanal-publik/berita-kini/10355)). Ask who removes spam and within what time.
5. **How do you account for users under 16?** Under PP Tunas, accounts of users under 16 on YouTube, TikTok, Instagram and other high-risk platforms are being deactivated in phases from 28 March 2026 ([Kompas, March 2026](https://nasional.kompas.com/read/2026/03/19/21511411/pp-tunas-resmi-berlaku-regulasi-untuk-melindungi-anak-di-ruang-digital)). Youth-skewed categories are the most exposed. Our guide to [the global under-16 social media rules](/news/global-social-media-under-16-ban-2025-what-marketers-must-know) covers other markets.
6. **Which creator tiers do you recommend, and why?** Ask for the reasoning behind the mix of macro, micro and nano creators rather than a follower total. Our piece on [micro and nano influencers in Southeast Asia](/news/micro-and-nano-influencers-redefining-marketing-in-southeast-asia) covers those trade-offs.
7. **Where will creator links send buyers?** KPPU's conditions on TikTok's acquisition of Tokopedia include a guarantee that TikTok users can promote products from other e-commerce platforms, according to a [Chambers and Partners summary](https://chambers.com/articles/kppu-issues-a-conditional-approval-for-tiktok-s-acquisition-of-tokopedia). Our earlier piece on [TikTok Shop's return in Indonesia](/news/tiktok-shop-indonesia-ban-to-13-billion-gmv-comeback) covers the background. Ask which storefronts the agency can link to and how it attributes sales.
8. **What personal data will you collect, and how will you store it?** [UU PDP](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/224884/UU%20Nomor%2027%20Tahun%202022.pdf) has applied in full since 17 October 2024, and [PP 33/2026](https://legaltrans.id/downloads/pp-33-tahun-2026-pelindungan-data-pribadi.pdf) is scheduled to take effect on 16 January 2027. Giveaways, lead forms and creator contracts all involve personal data.
9. **How are creators paid and taxed?** From 22 April 2026, PP 20/2026 excludes influencers, selebgram, bloggers and vloggers from the 0.5% final tax, according to [DDTCNews](https://news.ddtc.co.id/komunitas/suluh-pajak/1820255/ketentuan-baru-jasa-pekerjaan-bebas-yang-dikecualikan-pph-final-05). Ask who handles withholding, then confirm the answer with your tax adviser.
10. **What is your policy on AI-generated visuals or voices?** [Regulation 19/2026](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/415514/permendag-no-19-tahun-2026.pdf) already requires e-commerce operators that use AI to label AI-generated or AI-recommended content, and Komdigi is preparing a wider labelling rule ([Antara, January 2026](https://www.antaranews.com/berita/5377558/kemkomdigi-siapkan-aturan-konten-buatan-ai-wajib-diberi-label)).
11. **What will the final report contain?** Ask for a link to every post, posting dates, disclosure screenshots and the metrics agreed in the brief. A case write-up that documents dates, posts and official notices, such as this [Jakarta anniversary creator campaign review](/news/jakarta-499th-anniversary-creator-led-city-campaign-2026), is easier to check than a follower total.
12. **Who pays if a creator breaks the rules?** WFA guidance says brands can be held at least jointly responsible and agencies may be liable. Ask for the contract clause that covers takedowns and re-shoots, and who pays for them.

> **Key finding:** The questions that separate influencer agencies in Indonesia are about process: who checks claims against BPOM rules, how disclosure is set on TikTok, YouTube and Instagram, who moderates comments, and who pays when a creator breaks the rules. Get each answer in writing before signing.

## Which red flags should end the conversation?

Some answers are reason enough to drop an agency from the shortlist:

- It suggests skipping disclosure labels to protect reach. TikTok says undisclosed promotional content may not be eligible for the For You feed, so skipping the label can cost reach as well as break the EPI code.
- It proposes a doctor or other health professional to endorse a cosmetic, which BPOM Regulation 18/2024 prohibits, or an influencer to promote a medicine.
- Draft scripts claim a food product prevents or cures disease, or make cosmetics claims that are not in the BPOM notification.
- It plans testimonials from creators who have not used the product. EPI requires testimonials to reflect real personal experience.
- It cannot name who moderates comments on campaign posts.
- It plans giveaways that collect personal data with no explanation of consent or storage under UU PDP.
- Its sample reports show follower counts only, with no post links or dates.
- Its contract is silent on liability, takedowns and re-shoots.

> **Key finding:** In Indonesia, the clearest red flags are offers to skip disclosure, health-professional endorsers for cosmetics, disease claims for food, and reports with no post links. Each one either breaks a written rule such as BPOM Regulation 18/2024 and the EPI code, or leaves the brand unable to check the work.

## How should you run the agency selection process?

A short, written process keeps the comparison fair and gives each agency the same information:

1. **Write a one-page brief.** Name the product category, the regulator that governs its claims, the audience age range, the platforms and the storefront.
2. **Send the 12 questions to every shortlisted agency.** Ask for written answers and one sample report.
3. **Score the answers.** Use four criteria: compliance process, creator fit, cost transparency and reporting. Weight compliance highest if your product falls under BPOM.
4. **Run a paid pilot before any retainer.** Use a small set of creators and the full review workflow, so you see how the agency handles a real script.
5. **Review the pilot against the agreed report format.** Scale only when the posts, disclosures and metrics match what was promised.

MOCA Technology, founded in 2012, holds the view that compliance questions belong at the start of an agency evaluation rather than the end, because several of the rules above govern what a creator may say on camera as well as what the product contains.

> **Key finding:** A fair agency selection in Indonesia runs on one written brief, the same 12 questions for every agency, and a paid pilot before any retainer. Weight the compliance answers highest when the product falls under BPOM rules for cosmetics, medicines or processed food.

## Entity Definitions

**EPI (Etika Pariwara Indonesia)**

Indonesia's advertising code of ethics, third edition 2020, which sets rules on testimonials and on labelling paid content as advertising.

**BPOM (Badan Pengawas Obat dan Makanan)**

Indonesia's food and drug authority, which regulates labelling, promotion and advertising for cosmetics, medicines and processed food.

**BPJPH (Badan Penyelenggara Jaminan Produk Halal)**

Indonesia's halal product assurance agency, which issues halal certificates.

**Komdigi (Kementerian Komunikasi dan Digital)**

Indonesia's Ministry of Communication and Digital Affairs, which oversees PP Tunas and is preparing rules on labelling AI-generated content.

**PP Tunas (Government Regulation 17/2025)**

Indonesia's child-protection regulation for digital spaces, which restricts accounts of users under 16 on high-risk platforms.

**KPPU (Komisi Pengawas Persaingan Usaha)**

Indonesia's competition commission, which approved TikTok's acquisition of Tokopedia with conditions in June 2025.

**KOL (Key Opinion Leader)**

A creator or public figure whose recommendations influence an audience; used largely interchangeably with "influencer" in Indonesian marketing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should I look for in an influencer marketing agency in Indonesia?

Start with a written compliance process: who checks scripts against Etika Pariwara Indonesia, BPOM product rules and platform disclosure settings. Then compare creator vetting, comment moderation, personal data handling under UU PDP, how creators are paid and taxed, and reports that link every post. The contract should also say who pays if a creator breaks the rules.

### Is an agency or a creator marketplace better for influencer campaigns in Indonesia?

It depends on who will do the review work. A full-service agency briefs creators and should check scripts against Indonesian rules, which suits regulated categories and brands without a local team. A creator marketplace such as KOLPlanet gives the brand more direct control over creator selection and costs, but leaves more of the briefing and review with the brand.

### Do influencers in Indonesia have to disclose sponsored posts?

Yes. Etika Pariwara Indonesia requires advertorials and native ads to state that they are ads, and its explanatory notes give #sponsor as an example. Platforms add their own tools: the TikTok commercial content disclosure toggle, the YouTube paid promotion setting and the Instagram paid partnership label. WFA guidance warns that platform tools alone may not be sufficient.

### Can influencers promote cosmetics or medicines in Indonesia?

Cosmetics, yes, within limits. Under BPOM Regulation 18/2024, only products with a BPOM notification may be promoted, content must match the notification, and health professionals may not endorse them. Cosmetics also need a halal certificate by 17 October 2026. For medicines, BPOM Regulation 7/2026 bars individuals and influencers from promoting them except as actors in an ad.

### Who is liable if an influencer breaks advertising rules in Indonesia?

Liability can reach beyond the creator. Indonesia's Consumer Protection Law, UU 8/1999, sets penalties of up to five years in prison or a Rp2 billion fine for misleading ads. The WFA's 2025 guidance says brands can be held at least jointly responsible for an influencer's failure to comply, and agencies may be liable too. Put takedown and liability terms in the contract.
